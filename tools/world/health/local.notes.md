# Local health cover for a new-resident couple aged 45: notes

Written by AI on 2026.11.18 by model claude sonnet 5.5, as research helper for the agent that owns the world data. Nothing here is committed.

## What is in the files

- `local.curated.csv`: private plans, one or more rows per place (17 rows, 8 places). The note on each row says which row is the recommended one and which are alternatives or cross-checks.
- `local-statutory.curated.csv`: compulsory (or, where marked, voluntary public) premiums and contributions (12 rows, 12 places). Notes start with `kind=statutory`.
- `local.sources.csv`: 23 sources. Every file or page is saved in `cache/<source_id>/`. Where a site refuses curl (nhi.gov.tw, ccss.sa.cr, aia.com.sg, ramq.gouv.qc.ca) the saved file is the page text as a web fetch tool returned it, called `*.extract.txt`.
- `au_privatehealth.py`: rebuilds the Australian figures from the official open dataset (stdlib only, 8 seconds).

Amounts are in local currency as published, per month for the couple. Where a source is annual, the arithmetic is in `note`.

## The headline

A published local private premium table existed for only 8 of the 26 places: Australia, Brazil (a regulator's price band, thin), Hong Kong, Malaysia, Panama, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand. The other 18 have no private row: Argentina, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Georgia, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Morocco, Mexico, New Zealand, Oman, Peru, Taiwan, UAE, Uruguay, Vietnam. Twelve places have a statutory row. Seven have neither a private nor a statutory row: Argentina, Georgia, Indonesia, Morocco, Oman, Uruguay, Vietnam. Insurers there quote through forms or through brokers. I did not use broker blogs as figures. Where a blog gave a number I mention it in the country entry as a pointer, not as data.

Two limits on the private rows. First, "in-patient and out-patient" holds only where I say so. The Hong Kong VHIS plan, the Singapore plan, the Malaysian AIA plan and the Thai Chubb rider are hospital cover; general practitioner visits are not covered. Panama's Mapfre plan and Australia's combined policies include out-patient or extras. Second, tables are for new business at age 45 in standard health; none includes loadings for health conditions.

## Country by country

**Thailand.** Private: Chubb Life 3D Health Excellence, Thai-language sales kit (file dated February 2026). HX60 (THB 10m annual limit, room THB 6,000 a day), ages 41-45: man 32,760 + woman 33,610 = THB 66,370 a year = THB 5,531 a month (recommended). HX40 (THB 5m, room 4,000) is THB 4,173 a month. It is a rider that needs a main life policy, so the cost of that policy is extra. In-patient plus follow-up out-patient only. Allianz Ayudhya, AIA, Muang Thai and Bangkok Life quote through calculators; no static table found. Statutory: none for a non-working foreigner (the 30-baht scheme is for Thai nationals; Social Security needs Thai employment; official statute not read, secondary sources agree). The O-A visa insurance rule is in the earlier health notes.

**Malaysia.** Private: AIA Med Insure, plan P100 D500P (RM100,000 annual limit, RM500 deductible per policy year, in-patient and surgical, no out-patient), age 45 man RM2,048.43 + woman RM2,129.25 = RM4,177.68 a year = RM348 a month, before government tax. This is an entry-to-middle limit. Zurich's disclosure sheets show richer plans (RM500,000 limit, RM1,558 a year for a 45-year-old man) but give no female rate. Statutory: none; foreigners pay higher public-hospital fees.

**Vietnam.** No published local premium table found. Bao Viet's InterCare benefit table (the one I located) is on a site that refuses my fetches; PVI and Bao Minh show no table. Statutory: the 4.5% health insurance contribution applies to employed foreigners with a work permit and a contract of 12 months or more (Tuoi Tre, April 2026, secondary). A non-working foreign resident has no route in that I could confirm.

**Indonesia (Bali).** No published local premium table found. Statutory: BPJS Kesehatan is for foreigners who work six months or more (Perpres 82/2018, definition of participant; secondary sources). A Legal Indonesia article of 10 November 2026 says BPJS will stop self-paid enrolment for foreigners from December 2026 and will insure investor-KITAS holders (company share of IDR 10bn or more), work-KITAS holders and their families only through a company; retirement and remote-worker KITAS holders cannot join. That article and the others are secondary; I did not read BPJS's own notice. For a non-working couple, treat the statutory premium as none.

**Taiwan.** Statutory: NHI Category 6 (regional population), NT$826 a month per person; insured plus one dependant NT$1,652 a month (NHIA table in force since 2021-01-01, still shown in 2026 and in the NHIA Annual Report 2025-2026). Foreign residents join after six months of residence with a residence permit. Supplementary premium of 2.11% applies to dividends, interest and rent paid by Taiwanese payers when a single payment reaches NT$20,000; it does not apply to share-sale gains; whether foreign-source dividends are caught was not found. Private: none found. The Insurance Bureau's filings and insurers' rate tables for 實支實付 medical riders were not reachable by search.

**South Korea.** Statutory: NHIS, regional subscribers, floor KRW 158,630 a month per household (KRW 140,210 health + KRW 18,420 long-term care), official NHIS notice for 2026. This is a floor: NHIS calculates 7.19% of income plus property points and charges the higher of that and the floor. A household head with F-5 or F-6 status has no floor. Foreigners join after six months. I did not confirm whether foreign investment income is counted. Private: none found. Silson (실손) premiums are on the insurers' and the General Insurance Association's quote tools; no static table reached.

**Singapore.** Private: AIA HealthShield Gold Max Special A for foreigners (private hospitals, 'as charged'), non-dependants age 41-45: S$2,017 a year each, S$4,034 for the couple = S$336 a month including 9% GST. The leaflet is dated as at 1 October 2026. In-patient only; the leaflet does not state the deductible and co-insurance. Foreigners are not covered by MediShield Life. For citizens and permanent residents the Ministry of Health publishes a comparison of every integrated plan (saved from the MOH site): useful if the couple become PRs. Statutory: none for foreigners.

**Hong Kong.** Private: the government's VHIS site hosts every insurer's premium schedule. AIA Flexi Plan, Standard Private room, age 45: man HK$28,520 + woman HK$33,507.20 = HK$62,027.20 a year = HK$5,169 a month (recommended). Cheaper: AIA Flexi Ward HK$1,686 a month, and the minimum Standard Plan HK$852 a month. Manulife and Chubb ward plans (HK$954 and HK$1,911 a month) show insurers differ by up to double for the same age. VHIS is hospital-led cover: out-patient GP visits are extra. Premiums exclude the Insurance Authority levy and are not guaranteed. Statutory: none. I did not find an official statement of what a non-permanent resident pays at public hospitals.

**UAE (Dubai).** Statutory: the DHA Essential Benefits Plan circular (in force 2025-01-01) sets the index rate at AED 550 to 750 per member a year plus VAT. Two members at the top of the band are AED 125 a month before VAT. Written under Dubai's city code `CITY-292223`. Private: no insurer table found (Daman, AXA Gulf, Sukoon, Orient quote online). Broker sources say mid-range regional plans cost about AED 3,000 to 9,000 a person a year and rise by 40 to 60% after 45; I do not use that as a figure. Abu Dhabi runs a separate scheme, not researched.

**Oman.** Nothing found. Searches return the 2026 expatriate sick-leave insurance and the employer-paid Dhamani health scheme for private-sector employees; neither applies to a non-working investor couple, and no premium table exists in what I reached.

**Georgia.** Nothing usable found. Georgia's universal programme covers citizens. Private plans for residents (Imedi L, GPI, Aldagi, Ardi) show prices only in broker guides and on a bank product page that refuses my fetch. Visitor insurance (30,000 GEL minimum for entry since 1 January 2026) is a different product. Not used.

**Morocco.** Nothing found. The compulsory AMO is for employees, the self-employed and pensioners with income links; I found no route for a non-working foreign couple, and no insurer table (Wafa, AXA Assurance Maroc, Saham, RMA). A comparator site gives MAD 380 to 550 a month for 45 to 60; not used.

**South Africa.** Private: Discovery Health Medical Scheme 2026 contribution table (1 April to 31 December 2026), Classic Smart Comprehensive: main member R8,576 + adult R7,920 = R16,496 a month (recommended). Classic Comprehensive R19,529; Classic Priority R11,087; Bonitas BonClassic R15,309 (Bonitas 2026 page). Open schemes charge the same at every age (community rating), with no medical underwriting; late-joiner penalties for adults over 35 with no previous cover are not in the tables and I did not price them. Statutory: none.

**Mexico.** Statutory (voluntary): IMSS Seguro de Salud para la Familia, age 40-49 MXN 13,800 a year each, MXN 2,300 a month for the couple, from the December 2025 table (press copy; pre-existing conditions block entry; 2026 is about 4% higher per press). Private: no published age table for gastos médicos mayores (AXA, GNP, Mapfre, Metlife quote only; CONDUSEF offers a simulator).

**Costa Rica.** Statutory: CCSS scale for voluntary insured and independent workers, January 2026. On a declared income of CRC 1,125,000 a month (the US$2,500 rentista minimum at CRC 450 per US$) the health share is 6.24% = CRC 70,200 a month; with the pension share (7.53%) it is 13.77% = CRC 154,913. The rate applies to the whole declared income, not by slice. At CRC 2,238,555 and above, health is 10.69% (at least CRC 239,000). The couple is normally covered by one contribution (secondary source). Private: INS and other insurers' individual tariffs not found; the INS documents that surfaced are group policies.

**Panama.** Private: Mapfre Panamá Plan Salud, Solución Classic (plan 346), tariff version 2025-10-01: B/.132.51 a month per person at 45-49, couple B/.265.02 plus 5% tax. In-patient and out-patient, B/.350,000 lifetime limit, B/.500 deductible on out-patient items, Panama and Central America. Statutory: none found for a non-working foreign resident.

**Colombia.** Statutory: contributory health regime for an independent person or capital renter: 12.5% of a base equal to 40% of monthly income (net of costs), minimum one minimum wage (COP 1,750,905 in 2026), maximum 25. Floor: COP 218,863 a month. Worked example in the row (COP 20m income gives COP 725,000). The row is the floor; the income-dependent part is everything above it. Whether foreign-source capital income counts, and the presumed-cost rate (27.5% or 28.08%), are not confirmed. Private: no insurer publishes an age table (Colsanitas, Sura, Colmédica quote).

**Peru.** Statutory (voluntary): EsSalud +Salud Seguro Potestativo, open to any resident, foreign or Peruvian, not insured through an employer: S/138 a month per person at ages 30-59 (fee table copied by mac.pe; official gob.pe page confirms eligibility but not the amounts). Couple S/276, with the spouse's rate assumed equal. Three-month waiting period for non-urgent care (secondary sources). Private: Rímac, Pacífico and Mapfre publish benefit documents but no age tariffs (I read Mapfre's Plan Salud Total, 16 April 2026: benefits only).

**Brazil.** Private (thin): ANS, the regulator, publishes price bands for each age band and contract type (open data, updated 2026-11-01). For individual or family plans aged 44-48 the middle category `$$$$` runs from BRL 1,033.75 to 1,342.57 a month per person; midpoint for the couple BRL 2,376. This sorts market prices into categories; it is not one insurer's tariff. Statutory: none. The public SUS is free at the point of use, but I saved no official page for it, so there is no row.

**Chile.** Statutory: FONASA 7% of taxable income capped at UF 90, so at most UF 6.3 a month, CLP 260,508 at UF = CLP 41,350.42 on 2026-11-17 (Superintendencia de Salud page; UF from the Banco Central via mindicador.cl). A lower declared base lowers it. Many choose an ISAPRE plan instead (price = plan base price in UF x age and sex factor); no ISAPRE table captured. Private: none found.

**Argentina.** Nothing found. The Superintendencia de Servicios de Salud says prepagas must publish their price tables ('valores de planes' on sssalud.gob.ar), but that page returns 404 and the site is script-driven. OSDE, Swiss Medical and others publish no list. Blogs give OSDE plan 210 near ARS 290,000 a month at 30; not used.

**Uruguay.** No published mutualista fee table found. Official facts: MSP's July 2026 note caps individual-fee increases at 2.13%, and the FONASA capita for ages 45-64 is UYU 3,727.95 (men) and UYU 4,559.55 (women) a month, in the earlier health notes (cached in `cache/health-local-uy-msp`). I would not use the capita as the private-plan price. A comparison site lists CASMU at UYU 3,400 and Médica Uruguaya at 3,200 as add-on fees; not used.

**Australia.** Private: the official PrivateHealth.gov.au dataset (CC BY 3.0 AU), November 2026 release. New South Wales, two adults, no children, combined hospital and extras, open to new members: Silver Plus median A$654 a month over 399 products (quartiles A$570 to A$748); Gold median A$927 over 114 products. Before the government rebate (nil for a high-income couple) and before any Lifetime Health Cover loading (2% for each year after age 30 without hospital cover; the one-year grace for new arrivals is from memory and not saved). Prices are community rated and state-specific; I computed NSW only; `au_privatehealth.py ALL|VIC|QLD|WA|SA|TAS|NT` does the others. Statutory: Medicare for permanent residents living in Australia (Services Australia; the site refuses my fetches, so the saved file is a search extract). Funded by the Medicare levy, 2% of taxable income, and a surcharge of 1% to 1.5% for high earners without private hospital cover. I did not read the ATO thresholds. Overseas Visitors Health Cover applies to temporary visas and has no published table.

**Japan.** Statutory: Tokyo Koto ward FY2026 NHI, source id `health-local-jp-koto`, confirmed on the ward page and Tokyo's table (20 of 23 wards use the same rates). Fixed per-person part: JPY 47,600 + 17,600 + 17,800 (ages 40-64) + 1,873 = JPY 84,873 a year; two people = JPY 169,746 = JPY 14,146 a month. On top: 13.01% of the previous year's income after a JPY 430,000 deduction, capped at JPY 670,000 + 260,000 + 170,000 + 30,000 per person. A new arrival with no income record is billed the flat part first (ward newsletter). Investment income in a withholding account that is not declared is left out of the base. Written under `JP-13` (Tokyo prefecture) with the measured_for text saying wards. Private: none found. Medical riders (Aflac, Sony Life, Lifenet) publish fixed-benefit premium tables, but I could not reach an insurer's own table; price-comparison sites do not count.

**Canada.** Statutory: amount 0. New permanent residents qualify. Ontario has no waiting period, British Columbia waits the rest of the arrival month plus two months, Québec up to three months (private cover advised for the gap). No premium for the insurance itself in Ontario, BC or Québec; BC dropped MSP premiums on 2020-01-01. Ontario's income-tax Health Premium runs from C$0 to C$900 a person a year (taxable income over C$200,600) and is not linked to OHIP; Québec's compulsory drug-plan premium is C$0 to C$789 a person a year in 2026-27 unless the couple have a private drug plan. Other provinces not read. Private: no insurer table found. Provincial supplementary plans (Pacific, Alberta, Manitoba and Medavie Blue Cross) do not publish age-banded rates in what I reached; I will not use broker ranges.

**New Zealand.** Statutory: amount 0. Resident visa holders are eligible for publicly funded services with no waiting period, and the new regulations from 2026-12-01 leave residents unchanged. Private: Southern Cross, nib, Accuro and AIA publish no price list (a broker comparison says as much). A broker's sample quote for Southern Cross Wellbeing One is in the statutory row's note as a pointer only; it is not a figure.

## What I think you may have wrong or should check

- Statutory rows that are rules, not fixed fees: Korea (floor), Costa Rica (income rate), Colombia (floor), Chile (cap), Japan (flat part only). The row amount is the floor or cap I name; you add the income part.
- Peru and Mexico IMSS are voluntary. If you want only compulsory contributions, drop them.
- Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand private rows are hospital-led. If the rule needs out-patient cover in the figure, add an out-patient allowance or mark them partial.
- Brazil's row is a regulator price band, the weakest private figure here.
- Currency check: Costa Rica's row depends on CRC 450 per US$ (my assumption from a secondary guide), and Chile's on the UF value of 2026-11-17.
- Kind labels: every `note` starts `kind=local-private` or `kind=statutory`; the file a row sits in follows from that.
- Several sources refuse plain fetches (see the list at the top); every figure was read from a saved file or a saved text extract, none from memory, except where a row says "from memory".
